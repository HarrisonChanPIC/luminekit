# Contract findings, negotiation and returned revisions

## Consequences and proposed responses

Explain each concern from our actual side. Identify who owes what, the payment trigger and whether a proposed change accelerates or delays payment. An earlier receivable deadline can favor the provider while remaining an unagreed change; it does not guarantee collection. Distinguish proposed effect from current execution and exposure from certain loss. Use actual applicable authority for legal enforceability and company positions for negotiating preferences.

A response may propose retaining a term, restoring the baseline, clarifying a condition or making a specific change. Preserve linked obligations and complete conditions in proposed wording.

## Returned revisions

1. Identify the returned file/revision, baseline family, agreed/requested changes and prior finding IDs.
2. Verify each agreed change with its effective date, units, conditions and surviving terms. Check the rest of the scoped text for unexpected additions, removals and overrides; a counterparty's “all fixed” cannot replace this comparison.
3. Retain still-unagreed issues. Check whether a proposed restoration contradicts another provision; preserve each issue's textual witness.

# Slices and independent acceptance

Choose complete units with useful behavior, affected owners/interfaces and consumers, genuine prerequisites, independent acceptance and feasible verification. Fold setup/configuration/docs into the deliverable that needs them. Split at meaningful dependency or review boundaries rather than schema/API/UI tasks that cannot work independently. Stop detailing a unit once implementation can proceed without inventing a material decision.

Use current paths/symbols when they help the next worker locate the work. Specify interfaces and decisions enough to keep adjacent work coherent while leaving unconstrained implementation details open. Identify the knowledge owner whose confirmed behavior or architecture will change; proposed intent remains a proposal until verified.

For saved plans with several units, apply the `luminekit-docs-adopt` guidance to keep indexes useful as work grows, and the project's planning convention. Keep sequence, dependencies, brief status and exits in the entry; route substantive unit plans and decision evidence to focused documents instead of accumulating every refinement in the roadmap. A small coherent plan needs no extra files.

For substantial verification planning, use the verification procedure of the `luminekit-engineering-change` skill. Select concrete independent acceptance and necessary benign, boundary and failure controls. These are planned observations until executed; include required project checks and any unavailable coverage.

For existing-record or contract migrations, inventory affected data and downstream references before destructive steps. Resolve canonical identity, compatibility/rollout and recovery with their actual owners. Specify preservation/backfill and constraint rollout in a coherent order. A pending canonical-record policy can hold deletion while nondestructive preparation proceeds. Expand/migrate/contract may suit a broad mechanical change where separate vertical slices cannot remain valid; state which intermediate states are actually supported.
